# Fission Impossible — Theme & Creative Direction

## The Premise

**FISSION IMPOSSIBLE** is a cooperative party game set in the Maintenance Room of **NuHaus Nuclear**, a struggling nuclear power plant in an alternate-universe 1980s where the Cold War never ended, safety regulations are "suggestions," and the union contract guarantees a 15-minute smoke break even during a meltdown.

One player is the **Maintenance Technician** — trapped in a room full of malfunctioning equipment they were never properly trained to operate. The other players are **The Hotline** — a team of equally unqualified colleagues frantically flipping through outdated employee manuals, trying to talk them through preventing catastrophe.

The reactor is overheating. The phone cord only reaches so far. Corporate is denying everything. And somewhere, a middle manager is insisting this still counts as your lunch break.

**Welcome to NuHaus Nuclear. We're Glad You're Expendable.**

---

## Setting: NuHaus Nuclear Power Plant

### The Company

**NuHaus Nuclear** is a third-tier energy company that won the government contract to build this plant by being the lowest bidder. Their corporate motto is *"Powering Tomorrow, Today... Eventually."*

Founded in 1962 by industrialist Werner NuHaus (pronounced "NEW-house" but he insists it's German), the company has a spotty safety record that's been classified, declassified, reclassified, and finally just "lost in a filing cabinet somewhere."

**Corporate Culture:**
- Mandatory "Positivity Minutes" before each shift
- Employee of the Month gets a reserved parking spot (in the contaminated lot)
- The suggestion box was welded shut in 1974 after "the incident"
- All safety concerns must be submitted in triplicate to a department that no longer exists

### The Era: Alternate 1980s

This isn't quite our 1980s. In this timeline:

- The Cold War is colder. Nuclear paranoia is a lifestyle.
- Technology is analog, chunky, and beige. Everything has wood paneling.
- Computers exist but are distrusted. "The Machine" in HR has been "calculating payroll" since 1978.
- Smoking is allowed everywhere. ENCOURAGED, even. "Calms the nerves."
- Workplace safety videos are hosted by unsettling puppets.
- The Soviet Union may or may not have spies in the plant. Probably in Accounting.

**Aesthetic Touchstones:**
- Chernobyl (the event AND the HBO series)
- Brazil (1985 film)
- The Office meets Fallout
- Severance meets Dr. Strangelove
- Analog horror / local 58 vibes
- 1980s corporate training videos (unintentionally terrifying)

## The Maintenance Room

The game takes place entirely in **Maintenance Room 7-G** — a cramped, cluttered space that was supposed to be "temporary" when it was set up in 1971.

### Room Description

> The fluorescent lights flicker. They've always flickered. Request #4,892 to fix them is still "under review."
>
> The walls are institutional green — the specific shade that research shows "minimizes employee suicide ideation" according to a 1967 study that has since been discredited.
>
> A motivational poster hangs crooked on the wall: a cartoon atom with a thumbs up saying "YOU CAN DO IT!" The atom's smile is unsettling. Its eyes follow you.
>
> The room smells of burnt coffee, cigarette smoke, and something that might be ozone. Or might be worse.

### Key Room Elements

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **The Console** | A wall of switches, dials, gauges, and buttons. Half are labeled. Some labels are wrong. One is in Russian and nobody knows why. |
| **The Hotline Phone** | A beige rotary phone. The cord is exactly 4 feet long. You cannot see the console from where the phone reaches. |
| **The Manual Shelf** | Binders. So many binders. Most are out of date. Some are for different plants entirely. One is just someone's divorce paperwork. |
| **The Coffee Machine** | Older than most employees. Makes a sound like a dying animal. The coffee is somehow both burnt and lukewarm. |
| **The Window** | Looks into the reactor room. The glass is supposed to be radiation-proof. The sticker that said so fell off years ago. |
| **Employee of the Month Photo** | Same person for 47 consecutive months: Gary. Gary doesn't work here anymore. Nobody knows what happened to Gary. |
| **The Suggestion Box** | Welded shut. Scorch marks around the edges. |

## Tone & Humor

### The Comedy Triangle

**FISSION IMPOSSIBLE** lives at the intersection of three comedic forces:

```
                    ABSURDIST
                       /\
                      /  \
                     /    \
                    /      \
                   /        \
                  /__________\
           DARK              SATIRICAL
```

**ABSURDIST:** The situations are ridiculous. The solutions are illogical. The manuals are written by someone who clearly never saw the actual equipment.

**DARK:** People could die. Many have died. The plant newsletter has an obituary section. It's usually the longest section.

**SATIRICAL:** Corporate bureaucracy, workplace dysfunction, Cold War paranoia, blind faith in "the system" — all valid targets.

### Humor Guidelines

✅ **DO:**
- Mock corporate culture, bureaucracy, and management
- Include dark irony (the safety poster is itself a fire hazard)
- Use period-appropriate absurdity (asbestos as a GOOD thing)
- Let the horror be mundane (form 27-B must be filed before dying)
- Reference actual 1980s workplace dysfunction

❌ **DON'T:**
- Make light of actual nuclear disaster victims
- Punch down at workers (they're the heroes, trapped in a bad system)
- Use shock value without comedic purpose
- Break immersion with modern references

### Example Tone Moments

> **Manual excerpt:**
> "In the event of a Code Vermillion, locate the nearest emergency shutoff valve. If the valve handle is missing (see: Incident Report 1974-C), improvise using approved NuHaus-brand tools only. Using non-approved tools voids your life insurance."

> **Loading screen tip:**
> "Remember: Radiation exposure is cumulative, but so is your pension! Stay positive!"

> **Module flavor text:**
> "This pressure gauge was last calibrated in 1979. It was supposed to be recalibrated annually. The calibration technician retired. Then died. Then the position was eliminated for 'budget reasons.'"

## Characters & Voices

### The Maintenance Technician (Player 1)

You are whoever drew the short straw today. You might be:

- **New hire** — Day one. Your training video was "corrupted" but you signed the form saying you watched it.
- **Old timer** — You've seen things. You don't talk about the things. The things talk to you sometimes.
- **Transferred** — Used to work at NuHaus's "other" plant. The one that's now a lake.
- **Temp** — Technically employed by a staffing agency that technically doesn't exist anymore.

**Voice characteristics:**
- Increasingly frantic
- Dark humor as coping mechanism
- Occasional existential observations
- Growing resentment toward The Hotline

### The Hotline (Other Players)

You are the people on the other end of the phone. You have "the manual" — a collection of documents that were definitely written by humans who theoretically understood this equipment at some point.

**Voice characteristics:**
- False confidence
- Corporate jargon masking ignorance
- Occasional genuine attempts to help
- Bureaucratic deflection when unsure

### Background Characters (Referenced)

| Character | Role | Notes |
|-----------|------|-------|
| **Werner NuHaus** | Founder/CEO | Only appears on motivational posters. May be dead. Company denies this. |
| **Gary** | Former Employee of the Month | 47 consecutive months. Photos only. Fate unknown. Possibly ascended. |
| **The Safety Salamander** | Mascot | Puppet from training videos. Deeply unsettling. Rhymes everything. |
| **Doris** | HR | Voice only. Calls are sometimes patched through her. She's "checking on something." |
| **The Soviets** | Alleged spies | Running joke. Every unexplained malfunction is blamed on them. |

## Visual & Audio Direction

### Visual Aesthetic

**Color Palette:**
- Institutional green (walls)
- Beige (everything plastic)
- Orange and brown (70s holdovers)
- Warning yellow (overused, thus ignored)
- Radiation green (the bad kind)
- CRT blue (screens)

**Typography:**
- Employee manuals: IBM Selectric typewriter font
- Warning signs: Heavy industrial stencil
- Corporate communications: Awkward early desktop publishing
- Handwritten notes: Increasingly desperate cursive

**UI Elements:**
- CRT monitor aesthetic (scan lines, slight curve, phosphor glow)
- Physical switches and buttons (tactile, chunky)
- Analog meters and gauges
- Faded warning stickers
- Coffee stains on documents

### Audio Design

**Ambient:**
- Fluorescent light buzz (constant)
- Distant machinery hum
- Occasional alarming sounds that "are probably fine"
- The coffee machine (concerning)
- Geiger counter (intermittent, anxiety-inducing)

**Music:**
- Muzak-style hold music when "connecting"
- Distorted corporate jingles
- Tension builds through silence and ambient dread
- Success: ironic triumphant synth
- Failure: alarm, then cut to black, then smooth corporate voice

**Voice:**
- Training videos: Overly cheerful narrator
- Emergency announcements: Calm to the point of disturbing
- The Safety Salamander: Rhyming, unhinged

## Module Theming Concepts

Instead of "bomb modules," we have **Plant Systems** that are malfunctioning. Each requires the Technician to describe what they see while The Hotline figures out the correct procedure.

### Example Module Themes

| Original | Fission Impossible Version | Flavor |
|----------|---------------------------|--------|
| Wires | **Coolant Bypass Valves** | Color-coded pipes. The color codes were changed in '76 but not all the pipes were updated. |
| The Button | **Emergency Override** | Big red button. Does something. Manual has 4 pages on when NOT to press it. |
| Simon Says | **Control Rod Sequence** | Lights indicate rod positions. Must be adjusted in order. The order changes based on the reactor's "mood." |
| Keypads | **Containment Door Lock** | Symbols are faded. Some are in Cyrillic. Nobody knows why. |
| Memory | **Shift Log Verification** | Must reference previous shift logs. Previous shift didn't fill them out correctly. |
| Morse Code | **Backup Radio** | Main comms are down. Someone is transmitting something. Might be a warning. Might be the Soviets. Might be the overnight DJ. |
| Mazes | **Vent System Navigation** | Guide a maintenance drone through vents. Map is from before "the renovation." |
| Passwords | **Computer Terminal** | Password must be entered. IT changed the password policy. Old passwords were too "guessable" (they were all NUCLEAR). |
| Complicated Wires | **Circuit Breaker Panel** | Multiple factors determine which breakers to flip. Some factors are superstition. The superstition is correct. |
| Wire Sequences | **Fuel Rod Assembly** | Track rod positions across multiple panels. Cumulative exposure is "being monitored." |

### Needy Module Themes

| Original | Fission Impossible Version | Flavor |
|----------|---------------------------|--------|
| Venting Gas | **Atmosphere Control** | Computer asks if you want to vent. Usually yes. Sometimes asks leading questions. |
| Capacitor Discharge | **Pressure Release** | Hold valve to release pressure. Let go to actually do your job. |
| Knobs | **Reactor Alignment** | Knob must be in correct position at specific times. The "correct" position is based on superstition that is somehow always right. |

## Narrative Framework

### The Scenario

Each game session is one **shift** at NuHaus Nuclear. Something has gone wrong (as usual). The Maintenance Technician must work through malfunctioning systems while The Hotline provides guidance from the break room, where all the manuals are stored (along with everyone's lunches, which are "probably fine" despite the radiation warning signs).

### Escalation

As the game progresses:
- The ambient radiation increases (visual/audio cues)
- Corporate begins sending increasingly tone-deaf announcements
- The Safety Salamander may appear with "helpful" tips
- The situation becomes more absurd while stakes remain deadly serious

### Endings

**Success (Meltdown Averted):**
> "All systems nominal. Incident logged as 'Minor Fluctuation.' Your overtime has been denied. Please report to mandatory debriefing. Coffee will be provided. The coffee is mandatory."

**Failure (Meltdown):**
> *Screen goes white.*
> *Beat.*
> *Corporate jingle plays.*
> "NuHaus Nuclear: Powering Tomorrow, Today... Eventually."
> *Press any key to begin your next shift.*

**Easter Egg Ending (Discover Gary's Fate):**
> *Classified.*

## The Safety Salamander

The Safety Salamander deserves special mention as a recurring motif.

### Origin

Created in 1968 for the "NuHaus Safety Pals" training video series, the Safety Salamander is a puppet that teaches workplace safety through rhymes. The puppeteer retired in 1972, but the videos are still mandatory viewing.

### Characteristics

- Orange and yellow coloring (ironically, radioactive warning colors)
- Fixed smile that somehow looks different in every frame
- Rhymes ALL safety advice, even when the rhymes don't quite work
- Occasionally breaks the fourth wall
- May or may not be sentient

### Example Appearances

> **Training Video Clip:**
> "If you see a leak that's GREEN,
> Leave the room! Don't make a scene!
> Find a manager to tell,
> Before you start to feel unwell!"
> *[Salamander winks. It shouldn't be able to wink.]*

> **Loading Screen:**
> *Salamander appears.*
> "Taking too long? That's no good!
> Work much faster than you should!
> Time is money, friend, you see—
> And time is running out for thee!"

## Summary

**FISSION IMPOSSIBLE** takes the proven asymmetric communication gameplay of Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes and recontextualizes it through:

1. **Setting:** A dysfunctional 1980s nuclear plant instead of an abstract bomb
2. **Tone:** Dark comedy and corporate satire instead of pure tension
3. **Aesthetic:** Analog retro-futurism, institutional decay, Cold War paranoia
4. **Characters:** The hapless worker vs. the broken system
5. **Stakes:** Personal survival AND preventing catastrophe (same difference, really)

The result should feel like playing through a workplace horror-comedy where the real monster is bureaucratic negligence, but also there's actual radiation, and also your coworkers are reading from a manual that might be for a different plant entirely.

*"NuHaus Nuclear: We're Glad You're Expendable."*
